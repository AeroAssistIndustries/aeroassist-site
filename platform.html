Inside the aircraft

What makes a hybrid airframe different from the drone in the trunk.

Click any point on the aircraft to see what it does and why it matters on a call.

01

Hybrid gas-electric powertrain

A small combustion generator charges the flight battery in the air, so endurance is set by fuel rather than cell capacity. That's the difference between covering an incident and leaving in the middle of it.

Click any numbered point on the aircraft.

The dock station

The aircraft is only half of it. The dock is what makes it automatic.

A DFR program lives or dies on what a launch costs in people and time. The dock removes the crew, the drive and the setup.

Dock station · open and ready
01

Self-contained pad

Weather-sealed enclosure with a heated, precision-centering pad. Sits on a roof, a tower or a trailer, and needs power and a network connection rather than a building.

02

Automatic refuel and charge

The aircraft re-docks and services itself between calls. No technician, no battery-swap cart, no aircraft sitting unavailable while someone finds a charger.

03

Health checks before launch

A preflight check runs on every task: propulsion, links, sensors, fuel and airspace. A failed check pushes the call to the next-nearest aircraft automatically.

04

Networked as one fleet

Docks coordinate. Dispatch tasks a call, not an aircraft, and the network picks whichever airframe gets there fastest with enough fuel to stay.

Sensors and payload

What the aircraft carries, and when each one earns its weight.

EO — daylight camera

Stabilized optical zoom for reading plates, following a direction of travel, and giving command a plain view of the scene.

Every call

IR — thermal imaging

Finds people through darkness, smoke and light cover, and shows a fire's heat behavior before crews commit to an interior approach.

Night, search, fire

Spotlight

Lights a yard, a field or an alley from above so responders on the ground aren't working a scene by flashlight.

Night operations

Loudspeaker

Gives incident command a voice on scene before anyone is exposed — evacuation instructions, de-escalation or contact with a subject.

Standoff, evacuation

Strobes and position lights

Required for night flight and useful on scene: the aircraft becomes a visible marker other units can navigate toward.

Required at night

Payload release

A common attachment point for carried payloads, so an aircraft already overhead can put something on the ground before a unit arrives. Configuration and approvals are agency-specific.

Mission dependent

Mapping and scene capture

Orthomosaic capture for collision reconstruction, fire scenes and post-incident documentation, tied to the evidence record.

American built. Arizona proud. Mission first.

AeroAssist designs, fabricates and builds its aircraft in Phoenix, Arizona, for the agencies that protect American communities, under NDAA §848 supply-chain rules.

BUILT IN THE USA
NDAA §848 COMPLIANT
ARIZONA · EST. 2022

Airframes are designed, fabricated and assembled in Phoenix, Arizona. A limited number of components are sourced abroad where no qualifying domestic supplier exists. NDAA compliance refers to Section 848 of the FY2020 National Defense Authorization Act.

REGULATORY AND COMPLIANCE

  • NDAA §848CompliantSecure supply chain: no covered foreign-made drone components, so agencies can buy with federal and grant funds.
  • FAAPart 107 · COA · BVLOSPrograms fly under Part 107, a COA and a BVLOS waiver. We do the filing work with your agency.
  • FCCCertifiedRadio and electronics are FCC certified for sale and use in the United States.
  • CECE markedConforms with the applicable EU safety, health and environmental requirements.

Compliance status is shown as it stands today and is updated as certifications are granted.
